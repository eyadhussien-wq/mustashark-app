## 02 — Payment Model

Candidate architecture:

```text
Client
  ↓
Licensed Payment Provider
  ↓
Lawyer / Lawyer-controlled beneficiary
  ↘
   Mustashark platform fee through a separately authorized mechanism
```

Verify merchant/beneficiary of record, provider capability, split settlement, refunds, chargebacks, disputes, commission mechanics, and custody implications.

## 03 — Commission Models

Evaluate without assuming preference:

1. provider-native split settlement;
2. lawyer-paid platform/service fee;
3. periodic bank settlement;
4. permitted wallet/mobile-money rail;
5. invoice-based settlement;
6. another licensed-provider mechanism.

For each model record legal basis, provider support, tax/accounting, invoicing, refund/chargeback treatment, cross-border support, and custody implications.

## 04 — Geographic Expansion


Each jurisdiction receives an independent review of entity/registration, legal-service advertising/referral/matching, lawyer licensing, payment/custody, tax/VAT/e-invoicing, privacy/data transfer, consumer protection, settlement, disputes, and commission model.

The decision record must explicitly assign contracting, payment receipt, commission receipt, invoicing, refunds, data processing, and liability.

## 06 — Regulatory Classification Rules

Classification follows actual system behavior, not marketing terminology.

Verify:

- who initiates payout/refund;
- who can redirect funds;
- who controls balances;
- who receives/holds/transmits funds;
- where funds settle;
- whether Mustashark creates payment/custody/transmission/balance-control behavior.

A licensed payment provider does not automatically create an exemption for Mustashark.

## 07 — Internal Financial Records

Wallet-like balances remain **provisional internal entitlement/receivable/payable records** until their economic and regulatory meaning is verified.

Database labels must not be treated as proof of stored money, e-money, escrow, custody, or settlement authority.

## 08 — Required Evidence Before Decision

- entity legality and activity classification;
- banking eligibility;
- payment-provider eligibility;
- lawyer/client contracting;
- commission settlement;
- tax/accounting;
- AML/CFT applicability;
- privacy/data protection;
- cross-border obligations;
- consumer protection;
- professional-service boundaries;
- operational liability;
- exit/relocation/expansion strategy.
